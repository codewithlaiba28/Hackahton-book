# Quickstart Guide: Chapter 2 - Robot Anatomy & Core Concepts

## Chapter Purpose
This chapter provides a foundational understanding of robot anatomy, covering the essential components, classifications, key hardware, and fundamental concepts of robot motion. It aims to equip the reader with the basic vocabulary and conceptual framework necessary to delve deeper into the design, control, and application of robotic systems.

## How to Get Started

1.  **Identify Core Components**: Begin by understanding the four main components that make up any robot: sensors, actuators, end-effectors, and control systems.
2.  **Classify Robot Types**: Learn how robots are categorized based on their physical form or morphology, specifically mobile, manipulative, and humanoid robots.
3.  **Understand Hardware Functionality**: Familiarize yourself with the function and examples of various sensor systems (like LiDAR, cameras, IMUs, force/torque sensors) and actuators.
4.  **Grasp Motion Concepts**: Focus on the concept of Degrees of Freedom (DOF) and how it dictates a robot's movement capabilities, along with a basic introduction to kinematics.

## Learning Outcomes (What you should achieve)

Upon successful completion of this chapter, you should be able to:

*   Identify and describe the core components of robot anatomy.
*   Classify robots based on their morphology (mobile, manipulative, humanoid).
*   Explain the function of various sensor systems (LiDAR, cameras, IMUs, force/torque sensors) and actuators.
*   Define Degrees of Freedom (DOF) and provide examples of its impact on robot motion.
*   Understand the basics of kinematics for robot motion and control.
